# iOS app

`ios/`: a native iPhone and iPad app. Xcode 26 or later, iOS 26.1 or later, Apple Silicon. It is a **device** of its person's, like a Mac: it holds every workspace it joined as files of its own and syncs them in the background with the Mac's own engine (`engine/`), linked in as a static library.

## Rules

- **Three tabs: Browse, Recent, Search** (`WorkspaceView`, one `NavigationStack` each, held by `Shell`). Browse, the first and where the app opens, is the workspace as one tree, folders opening in place, in the Mac sidebar's sections (`TreeModel`), under the workspace's name (the switcher, and the way to its settings); Recent is what changed, newest first (`cloud_recent`, `RecentModel`); Search is `WorkspaceFiles.search` over the sections, names first, then the words of notes. A document pushes over the tab it was opened from, and a link tapped in a note onto that note's stack; a link to a page from outside opens over Browse, whatever tab was showing. On an iPad (`.sidebarAdaptable`) Browse's tree sits beside the document.
- **Browse is three headings, each its folder at the top of the workspace**: Library (what everyone has), My files (this person's own, where what they share stays) and Shared with me (what others shared with them), in that order and always there, as in the Mac's sidebar; the three folders are never rows of their own. Anything else at the top, and what is straight under Shared with me that nobody shared, is in no section (`Place.nowhere`): it stays on this iPhone, and the top's strays show in a group of their own only when there are any. The names are `TreeModel.libraryFolder`, `myFilesFolder` and `sharedFolder` (⇄ `LIBRARY_DIR`, `MY_FILES_DIR`, `SHARED_DIR`, engine/src/cloud/space.rs and src/cloud.ts; `LIBRARY_FOLDER`, `MY_FILES_FOLDER`, `SHARED_FOLDER`, cloud-worker/src/layout.ts: **change all four**).
- **New things land in Library**: Browse's New note and New folder, and a move's first choice. A move never offers a folder in no section, and nothing is made at the top or straight under Shared with me. Library, My files and Shared with me are never renamed, moved, removed or shared (`FolderModel.isSection`).
- **Who can open a path is read off the status, as the Mac reads it**: `Sections.place` ⇄ `placeOf`, `Sections.share(at:)` ⇄ `shareAt`, `Sections.documentStem` ⇄ `documentStem`, `Sections.audience` ⇄ `audienceLine`, `Sections.wider` ⇄ `widerAudience` (src/cloud.ts, **change both**). A shared document's `SpaceStatus.path`, and the path a `cloud-space-left` names, has no extension: its markdown, its html and its comments are one document.
- **Share is one sheet** (`ShareSheet`, `ShareModel`), on a folder or a document in My files or in something shared, whether or not it is shared yet (`cloud_share`, with the absolute path; `cloud_share_member`); its words are the Mac's Share dialog's (`ShareWords` ⇄ src/ShareDialog.tsx, **change both**). It is offered on a row, and beside the line above a note's title that says who can open it (`AudienceLine`).
- **Something shared is not removed or moved like the rows beside it.** Whoever can open the folder it sits in (`placed`) moves it, and deletes it for everyone (`cloud_share_delete`); anyone else leaves it, or copies it to My files (`WorkspaceFiles.copy`, which leaves `.doklin` behind, as the Mac's `copy_recursive` does: **change both**). A folder that holds something shared is not removed around it: each is deleted for everyone first, or the folder stays. A move is asked first when it lets more people open the thing, or takes something shared into Library, where the share ends (`Sections.moveQuestion` ⇄ `confirmMove`, `Sections.deleteQuestion` ⇄ `deleteShare`, src/App.tsx: **change both**).
- **What is open follows something shared** when it leaves or moves. `cloud-space-left` closes every screen of it or inside it (`Workspace.left`, `ShareLeft.holds`), and is told as news unless this person gave it up here (`Workspace.giveUp`). `cloud-space-moved` — sync moved it where it sits, or renamed it as somebody renamed it — is followed (`Workspace.relocated`): the tree's open folders, a note's history and a store's moments by their routes, and an open document in place, under the route it was opened by (`DocumentModel.follow`; `Shell.now` says where that screen's document is now).
- **A link opens its page, or waits for it.** `Workspaces.follow` asks the engine which workspace the link is in (`cloud_link_target`): one this device has not joined says how to join; otherwise Browse holds the page's place (`arrivingPage`, `ArrivingView`) and asks `cloud_follow_link` again as sync brings files and every few seconds, for `arrivalWait`, then shows the engine's sentence — which never tells a deleted page from one in a folder this person is not in.
- **Who changed something is said in words** (`WhoWords`): a person is their name, this person *You*; an agent is its person's first name and the name they gave it, "Maya’s Research agent" — the whole name when two people share the first, as it stands when it already says whose, "An agent" when the directory no longer has it. The directory is `/api/meta`'s, agents with their `person` (cloud-people-agents.md *Agents*).
- **What was read is the device's own** (`SeenStore`, `seen.json` beside the engine's files): the content address a document's files held when it was last shown, by file id (by path until it has one). Recent's dot is a file whose hash differs — or, never read, one changed since the store began, so a phone that just joined does not call everything new. A change of this person's own is never unread. Opened again, what changed since is worked out against the version read (`versions_history`: the manifest's hash is a prefix of the version's), block by block (`ChangeMarks`), and marked. *Remove from this iPhone* forgets it, with what was open in the tree and the recent searches (`UserDefaults`, by `wsId`). A workspace just joined shows before the engine has said its id, so the tree keeps what is open by its folder until then and moves it to the id (`TreeModel.settle`).
- **Sync speaks only when it needs you.** Its quiet states (`Synced`, `Syncing…`, offline, paused) are a line under the workspace's name on Browse; a hold, a question, an error, a notice or a toast rises into the one bar above the tabs (`NeedsYouBar`, `tabViewBottomAccessory(isEnabled:)`, the reason for iOS 26.1). A document hides the tab bar, and its toasts show in its own bar.
- **Reading first; editing in place, with the Mac's own editor.** A document opens to read; *Edit*, or a double tap on a block, makes it editable right there, at that block (`NoteEditing`, `NoteEditor.swift`). The editor is the Mac's Milkdown set-up (`src/ios-editor/`), so the phone writes the markdown the Mac writes: the frontmatter is split off and put back byte for byte (`src/frontmatter.ts`), comments are expanded with their bodies from the meta file and written back as bare markers (`src/metaFile.ts`), and a note whose comments the editor would lose opens to read only. Links to documents are the Mac's too (`src/linkMark.ts`): the editor is handed where the note is and `Workspace.documents`, again whenever they change, and hands back the note with the link records its sidecar is to hold; a relink (a rename while the note is open) is not an edit. Opening writes nothing: a save is measured from what the editor first had. There is no plain-markdown view. The bridge is `window.doklin` there and the `doklin` message handler here (**change both**).
- **A note's title is its name**, as on the Mac ([architecture.md](architecture.md) *The title*): the reader shows the file's name above every note, and the editor edits it there (`src/ios-editor/title.ts`). The page hands a title over a moment after typing stops and when the title is left; the app renames the file (`DocumentModel.rename`: rendition and sidecar along; a name another document in the folder has, its markdown or its html, is refused in words) and answers with the name now (`named`). The open note, its comments and what it is said to be editing follow the new path. New note makes "Untitled", empty (`DocTitle.firstFree`, ⇄ the Mac sidebar's New Document), and opens it in the editor with the caret in its title (`Route.made`).
- **A save from the editor writes the sidecar first** (`DocumentModel.save(_:links:over:)`): the sidecar with the records of both texts, then the text, then the sidecar with the new text's records alone (`MetaFile.withLinks`, as the Mac's `sidecarWhileWriting`); everything else in the sidecar is kept as it is. A sidecar that is there but can't be read stops the save before anything is written.
- **The editor reaches nothing.** It is served from the app's own scheme (`doklin-editor://app/`): the editor's files from the app, the note's pictures (`/doc/<path>`) from this iPhone, nothing else; a content rule list blocks every web load. One web view, made after the first frame and kept (`EditorPage.shared`): one note is edited at a time.
- **The phone runs the engine; Swift draws the screens.** Swift calls the engine's commands by name with the Mac frontend's arguments (`Engine.shared.call`, `engine/src/command.rs`) and hears its events; nothing in Swift talks to a domain. What the engine answers is mirrored in `Payloads.swift` (⇄ `engine/src/cloud/status.rs` ⇄ `src/cloud.ts`, **change all three**).
- **A workspace is joined with a line**: `Add a device` on a Mac, or an invite (`cloud_parse_invite` → `cloud_redeem` → `cloud_join`). It is downloaded to `Documents/<name>`; *Remove from this iPhone* is `cloud_leave` (signs out, deletes the copy, nothing changes in the cloud).
- **The sign-in is in the Keychain**, by workspace id (`kSecAttrAccessibleAfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`): readable while the phone is locked, never in a backup. The engine asks for it through `Host::secrets` (two C callbacks); the phone's `cloud.json` holds `token: ""`. A Keychain that cannot be read just now is never taken for "no sign-in": that workspace waits, untouched (`CloudFile::unread`).
- **Nothing of a workspace is in a backup**: `Application Support/Doklin` (the engine's files, the device id) and `Documents` (the copies) are excluded. A restored phone has nothing, and joins again as a new device.
- **Nothing written here is lost quietly.** A save writes only over what the note held when it was opened (`DocumentModel.save(_:over:)`); a change sync made meanwhile is a choice for the person. *Remove from this iPhone* syncs first and asks again when something has not reached the domain. A line's code works once: a join whose download failed retries with the sign-in it bought.
- **The sync line is the engine's phase, in the Mac's words** (`Limits.swift` ⇄ `syncWords` in `src/cloud.ts`, **change both**). While sync runs (`idle`, `syncing`) the day's limits (`usage.mode`) take over: `bulk-waits` with changes waiting says "412 changes wait until 05:30" (`resumesAt`, local time), with *Send now* (`cloud_lift_wait`) when `usage.liftable`; after a lift, "Sending 412 changes…" while `usage.lifted` and `waiting > 0`; `slow` says sync is slower. A fleet cause has no button: nobody lifts it. `limited` shows the engine's `error` as it stands. `pending-adds` is a notice over the line with *Sync them* / *Not now* (`cloud_confirm_adds`, `sync` true or false), worded from the last `cloud-pending-adds` event.
- **The engine polls the workspace a person is looking at most often**: `woke`, `settle` and a change of workspace tell it `cloud_set_focus {window: "main", folder: <the selected workspace's root, or null>, focused: <the app is active>}`. Nothing polls while iOS has the app suspended.
- **The Cloudflare plan (`usage.plan`) is a row of the workspace's Settings**: read-only, and the superadmin's (`workspace.manage` in `can`) to change, asked first (`cloud_set_plan`). Hidden until the domain has said.
- **Every write is the app's own, then `touched`.** There is no folder watcher on iOS: a file written without `Workspace.wrote` (which rings the engine's edit bus) syncs only at the next poll. `WorkspaceFiles` writes; `Workspace.wrote` tells the engine and every screen. A rename or a move goes through `Workspace.moved`, which tells the engine which file or folder went where (the `moved` command, absolute paths) before `wrote`: a note renamed and typed on at once stays the same document, and something shared stays that share.
- **No Trash.** A deletion on the phone — by a person or by sync — is outright; the version store keeps what went (Recently deleted).
- **What a folder holds is the Mac sidebar's rule**: a document is a stem (markdown, html, or both), `<stem>.meta.jsonl` goes wherever its document goes and is never a row, hidden names are `isHiddenOrIgnored` (⇄ `is_hidden_or_ignored` in `engine/src/lib.rs` ⇄ the worker's `IGNORED_NAMES`, **change all three**).
- **Leases are honoured**: a note another device holds opens view-only, with *Take over*; the editor says what it is editing (`cloud_set_activity`), never the reader.
- **What a person may do is the worker's role table** (`CloudStatus.can`, `Workspace.may`); empty means not known yet, and the engine refuses in words.
- **Comments are written as the Mac writes them**: bare markers in the markdown, bodies in `<stem>.meta.jsonl`, byte for byte — sync merges that file by its records with a Mac's (`engine/src/sidecar.rs`). `Comments/` ports `src/criticMarkup.ts`, `src/metaFile.ts`, the worker's placement rule and its reading of an html page (`cloud-worker/src/htmlText.ts`: how much of a thread's place the file has), held to `verify-harness/comments-fixture.json` with the TS (**change both**; for a page's reading the app's script is the third, `src/htmlBridge.ts`). A comment on a page is started on a Mac. Entries are stamped to the second; an emptied meta file is emptied, never deleted. The author is the engine's `device_name`.
- **Tags are the Mac's** ([architecture.md](architecture.md) *Tags*): `Markdown/Tags.swift` ports `src/tags.ts`, held to `verify-harness/tags-fixture.json` with it, the Mac's Rust and the engine (**change all four**). The list is the engine's status's (`CloudStatus.tags`, `Workspace.tagList`), changed through the engine (`cloud_set_tag`, `cloud_remove_tag`), which refuses what the worker would in the same words — how many values a tag has and how long what they mean is are its alone to refuse — and the status is read again after each change. Settings' *Tags* lists them (`TagsView`), with *New tag* and *Edit*, the sheets a note's chips open too (`TagSheetView`). The chips sit right under the title, above who changed it (`TagsRow`); a note with no tags has no row, and its first is added from More → *Add tag*. A tag change is a save of the note (`DocumentModel.setTag`): a change sync made meanwhile is read first, and the tag set on it. A rename puts the new name or values in the list beside the old (an old name at the end, while the list has room), rewrites every note in the sections on this iPhone that has the tag, and then takes the old out (`Workspace.renameInNotes`). Read-only where the note is: another device holds it, this person may not work, a version — and while the editor is open, whose page draws the chips from the block it holds and takes a block changed elsewhere while the body wasn't (`window.doklin.head`, `NoteEditing.adopt`), so a save there never undoes a tag set on another device; its chips follow the list (`NoteEditing.tagsChanged`). Search splits its tokens off (`Tags.searchQuery`); with tags and no words, every note that has them is a result, by name.
- **A folder can be a board, as on the Mac** ([architecture.md](architecture.md) *Boards*): `Markdown/Boards.swift` ports `src/boards.ts` and `src/rank.ts`, held to `verify-harness/boards-fixture.json` with them and the engine's merge (**change all three**); sync merges the file, so nothing here does. A folder holding a `boards.json`, in any case, is a board row: its name pushes the board (`BoardView`, `BoardModel`), its chevron opens the folder in place, and the file is never a row (`WorkspaceFiles.list`). A folder's menu has *Show as Board…* (`ShowAsBoardSheet`: a tag of the list, or *New tag…*) or *Show as Folder*, which removes the file (no Trash: history keeps it). A note's tags are read off this iPhone's copy, never asked for. Every write of `boards.json` is made on the file as it is just then (`Workspace.changeBoard`), and never over one this app can't read; a card moved to another column writes its note first (`Workspace.rewrite`, as a chip sets a tag). A rename or move made here keeps a card's key in the same step (`Workspace.moved` → `followCards`), and a tag's rename regroups every board grouped by it, after its notes (`regroupBoards`).
- **Rules the Mac's frontend states are ported, not re-invented**, and both sides say so: `timeAgo`/`timeUntil` (`Clock`), `widerAudience` (`Sections.wider`), `mayInviteAgain`, `buildAgentLoginPrompt` (`AgentPrompt`), the Agents and Published pages screens' words about sections. An address (slug) is checked as the Mac checks it; the engine has the last word.
- **History has no Trash either**: a moment's restore removes what the Mac would move to the Trash, so the moment kept before it is pinned (never thinned) and what went stays under Recently deleted. Recently deleted and the timeline read this iPhone's own stores, since it joined: one per section, Library's first (`stores(of:)`); the workspace's root keeps none, and a restore lands in its store's folder. A store is called by where its section is (`VersionsStatus.root`): its folder, or, for a shared document's, the document's path without its extension — and that store names the document's files as its section does, which may not be the name they have on this iPhone (`Workspace.path(_:in:)`). The timeline offers the folders' stores (`folderStores`); a shared document's history is its own versions. Recently deleted also lists what was shared and deleted for everyone, for whoever may restore it (`DeletedShare`: a `SpaceStatus` with `deletedAt`, `placed`; `cloud_share_restore`). A note's versions also include what other devices mirrored.
- **A note's links to documents read as the Mac resolves them** (`DocLinks.resolving`, rules: [cloud.md](cloud.md) *Links*): one its sidecar files opens that file wherever it is now, a named link shows the file's name now, and one that opens nothing is greyed and a tap says the engine's one sentence. `DocumentModel.document` is the resolved note, so Find and the marks of what changed read what is shown; it is resolved again whenever `Workspace.documents` (`cloud_documents`) changes.
- **A link to a page opens the app** (rules: [cloud.md](cloud.md) *Links*): from doklin.cc straight, by `applinks:doklin.cc` in `Doklin.entitlements`, or as `doklin://` (`Info.plist`), both through `onOpenURL` → `Workspaces.follow`, whose page the workspace's screens open (`opening`). A tap on one in a note stays in the app (`Workspaces.isPageLink`): one of the note's workspace on this iPhone pushes onto the note's stack (`Workspace.page`), any other goes through `Workspaces.follow`.
- **The engine's sentences say what this device is** (`Setup::device_kind`, `UIDevice.current.model`: iPhone, iPad).
- **Callouts are read as the Mac reads them**: a top-level quote whose first line is `[!NOTE]` (or `TIP`, `IMPORTANT`, `WARNING`, `CAUTION`), alone, any case, is `Block.callout` (`CalloutKind.marker` ⇄ `parseCalloutMarker` in `src/calloutTypes.ts`, **change both**); the reader's box (`CalloutView`) and the editor's (`src/ios-editor/editor.css`) look alike, and so do the reader's headings (`Font.docHeading`) and the editor's.
- **A flowchart is the Mac's picture, drawn natively.** The reader runs the Mac's own renderer (`src/flowchart/`, as the script `Editor/flowchart.js`) in a JavaScript context with no page, off the main thread (`FlowchartRenderer`), for the column the block is in: its width in points, less as the person's text size grows, so a picture's words are the size the renderer set and grow with the note's. What comes back is a scene (`Scene.swift` ⇄ `src/flowchart/scene.ts`, **change both**; both held to `verify-harness/flowchart-fixture.json`), which `FlowchartView.swift` draws item by item in the `--dg-*` colours. Another kind of ` ```mermaid ` diagram, and a flowchart that cannot be read, is its source under the renderer's sentence; a ` ```svg ` drawing is its source.
- **Colours are generated, never typed**: `node scripts/ios-colors.mjs` writes every colour set in `Assets.xcassets` from `src/styles/tokens.css`, for the names `Theme.swift` asks for. `--check` runs in CI.
- **`ios/Doklin/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` lists every "required reason" API the app calls, the engine's Rust included** (UserDefaults; file timestamps, which Rust's `stat` counts as): App Store Connect checks each upload's binary against it. A new one (a free-space check, `mach_absolute_time`) is an entry there too.
- **`DoklinKit` imports nothing Apple-only** outside `#if canImport(…)`, so its tests run on Linux. Views go in `ios/Doklin/`; logic a view needs goes in the kit.
- User-facing errors are sentences, here too: the engine answers a refusal as a phrase (`that code doesn't work`), and `Engine` makes it a sentence where it comes in (`DoklinError.sentence`); screens show it as it stands.

## Traps

- iOS moves the app's folder on an update: `Setup::anchor` is the container, and everything the engine writes down is relative to it (`engine/src/paths.rs`). The selected workspace is remembered by `wsId`, never by path.
- `cloud_join` answers the folder before the engine has sent that workspace's status: `Workspaces` keeps a just-joined root selected until it does.
- ONE-TIME, to be deleted with the engine's `from_library` (engine/src/cloud/layout.rs): a workspace from the layout before sharing in place has its `Private/` and `Restricted/` renamed as the engine starts, and what this iPhone stored by path follows before any screen reads it (`Workspaces.followLayoutMoved`, asked of `cloud_layout_moved`: the tree's open folders, and what was read of a file with no id yet — `Sections.fromLibraryLayout` ⇄ `moved_rel` ⇄ `fromLibraryLayout`, src/cloud.ts, **change all three**).
- The app gets about thirty seconds when it leaves the screen: `settle` flushes versions and syncs every workspace inside a background task. Nothing syncs while iOS has it suspended.
- `UIDevice.current.name` is the generic model name ("iPhone") without Apple's user-assigned-name entitlement: that is what Devices, presence and comment authors show.
- The bar above the tabs is the tab bar's accessory: a screen that hides the tab bar (a document) hides it too, so a document draws its own toasts. The accessory's content keeps no state of its own (iOS 26.1 drops it on a tab switch): its questions are asked from `WorkspaceView` (`SyncQuestions`).
- WebKit puts its own bar over the keyboard (previous, next, done); the editor's content view is made a subclass of itself without one (`dropInputAccessory`).
- A note's top-level blocks are counted by two parsers, the reader's and Milkdown's: Edit names the block by index and its first words, and the editor finds it by the words when the counts differ.
- `Info.plist` and `Doklin.entitlements` stay outside `ios/Doklin/`: inside the synchronized folder a file is copied as a resource too, and the build fails on two commands producing one file.
- Apple reads the association file from its own cache, refreshed when the app is installed: a change to it reaches phones only once doklin.cc is deployed (`site.yml`, on the push to `main`), and then only on the next install. The archive that first carries the entitlement makes Xcode add *Associated Domains* to the App ID.
- `"\r\n"` is one `Character` in Swift. Lines are split by `MarkdownLines.split`, and a ticked box changes one character in place, so a note's line endings survive.
- A block's `line` is a line of the file, frontmatter included: `togglingTask` takes that number as it is.
- `x-doklin-client` is `ios/<MARKETING_VERSION>+<build>` (`AppVersion`): the build is the number Xcode gave the upload, so the pair is what TestFlight shows, and what `MINIMUM_IOS` names (*Mandatory releases*, [release.md](release.md)). A Debug build says `+dev`, which no domain turns away: its number is the project's `1`, never one TestFlight gave. The version is the project's, not the Mac app's: `bump` does not stamp it, and a push touching only `ios/` cuts no release.
- A domain that turns this build away (`app-outdated`) gets `UpdateRequiredView` over every screen, its one button opening TestFlight (`itms-beta://`): an app cannot update itself. It covers what is under it rather than replacing it, so an edit on its way is still saved; a sheet already open stays above it until it is closed.
- A flowchart's words are measured in Inter (`src/flowchart/interWidths.ts`) and drawn here in the system font, which sets a line within two points of Inter's width: a box's own margin takes the difference. Another font for figures needs that measured again.
- The flowchart script runs where there is no `window`, `document` or `console`: a dependency that asks for one at load breaks every flowchart on the phone, and only `FlowchartTests` (on a Mac) and the app's journey say so.
- The UI tests find rows and fields by `accessibilityIdentifier` (`entry-<name>`, `recent-<name>`, `result-<name>`, `line`, `join`, `edit`, `done`, `workspace-menu`, `workspace-settings`, a board's `column-<value>` — `none` for no value — `card-<name>`, `board-new-<value>` and `board-menu`, …), tabs and buttons by their label: renaming either is a change to the tests too. A row of the tree is one element, whose label is its name and the line under it ("Shared with 1 person"); a board folder's row is two, its chevron (`chevron-<name>`) and the rest. In the editor, the note is the web view's text view labelled *Note*, and its title the one labelled *Title*.
- The tree is longer than a screen, and a list draws only the rows near what shows. A row further down does not exist for a UI test until it is scrolled to; one an earlier scroll left under the navigation bar takes no swipe or long press; and a tap on Browse's tab while its tree shows scrolls the tree to its top. A journey reaches a row with `reveal` (`UITestCase.swift`), and tells two rows of one name apart by what their labels say, never by counting them. A list scrolled down also shrinks the tab bar to the tab in front (`tabBarMinimizeBehavior`): the other tabs are not there until it is opened out, which `tab` does.
- A field in an alert carries no `accessibilityIdentifier`: a UI test finds it as the alert's one field.
- A share that holds nothing is erased when it is deleted: a journey that restores one puts a note in it first.
- iOS 27 changes what the UI tests meet: a button inside a menu's `Section` loses its `accessibilityIdentifier` (a menu's items are found by their label), a confirmation dialog is a popover with no Cancel, drawn wherever iOS puts it (shut with a tap outside it), and a tap on an alert's button just after typing into its field can be lost (`answerAlert` taps again). On any iOS a tap on a toast's button can be lost too, the toast left as it was: a journey taps it again while it is still offered (`undo`).
- SwiftUI's drag and drop sessions (`onDragSessionUpdated`, `onDropSessionUpdated`) are iOS 27: a board drags with `.draggable` and a `DropDelegate` per column, which say nothing when a drag is put down off every column. A drag no column has heard of for a moment is taken as over (`BoardView.endUnheard`). A card held for its menu is lifted too, so nothing on the board changes until a column hears the drag.
- A horizontal `ScrollView` runs under the navigation and tab bars but keeps none of its content clear of them: the board measures the safe area outside it and pads its columns by it.
- A card's menu held open (a view both `.draggable` and with a `.contextMenu`) leaves the UI tests waiting 60 seconds for the app's animations at each step until the board is left, though the app is idle: `BoardUITests` holds a card for its menu once, last.
- The engine is built for Apple Silicon only. The project excludes `x86_64` from simulator builds (`EXCLUDED_ARCHS`).
- The simulator build must stay signed-to-run-locally for the UI tests: with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO` the app has no Keychain, and a sign-in cannot be kept.

## TestFlight

The app reaches phones through TestFlight only, never the App Store.

- A build goes up from Xcode: Product → Archive, then Distribute App → App Store Connect, with *Manage Version and Build Number* on. Every upload needs a higher build number than the last, and Xcode takes the next one; nothing in the repository counts them. The same from the command line, with the account Xcode is signed in to: `xcodebuild archive -allowProvisioningUpdates`, then `xcodebuild -exportArchive -allowProvisioningUpdates` with export options `method` app-store-connect, `destination` upload, `manageAppVersionAndBuildNumber` true.
- Archiving needs one device registered to the team, even though nothing is installed from Xcode: automatic signing signs the archive for development first, and Apple makes no development profile for a team with no devices. Xcode registers a connected iPhone only with Developer Mode on; its UDID added under Devices on developer.apple.com does the same without it.
- `MARKETING_VERSION` stays put between builds: testers outside the App Store Connect team see a build only after Apple's beta review, which runs once per version.
- To make a build mandatory: once it is on TestFlight, Run workflow on `release` with *iphone* set to the build as TestFlight shows it (`0.1.0 (57)`), then update the worker. Every build older is turned away from then on, so never name a build that is not out. On the App Store, where every release has a version of its own, the minimum is a version alone (`1.2.0`), and the cover's button has to open the App Store page instead.
- A build expires 90 days after it is uploaded, and the app stops opening: a newer one goes up at least that often.
- Export compliance is answered in the project (`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO`): the app's only encryption is HTTPS to its workers.

To try the app in the simulator with no domain: `node verify-harness/serve-worker.mjs --no-app`, mint a device line with the superadmin token it prints (`drive-ios.mjs` shows how), and paste it.
