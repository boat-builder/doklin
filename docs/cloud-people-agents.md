# Cloud: people and agents

Routes, tables, status codes: [cloud-worker/README.md](../cloud-worker/README.md). Sync rules: [cloud.md](cloud.md).

## Credentials

- **Every credential is a row.** No device is matched any other way, so none is special. A database that cannot answer is `503` for every bearer, never `401`: a `401` is what stops a Mac for good.
- **The key** (`OWNER_TOKEN`) is nobody's sign-in. As a bearer it is answered on `GET /api/meta` (no `you`) and `POST /api/auth/recover`, and `403` on every other route. The recover trades it for a device token as the superadmin and spends it in the same batch, first (`spent_keys`, by sha256; a wipe keeps it), so a copy left in an agent's chat lets nobody in.
- **The superadmin** is the one person the `superadmin` table names: whoever last traded a key, which only the Cloudflare account can set. A trade naming another email moves the role to them — the only way it moves; nothing in the app hands it over — and the one before keeps `owner` on their row. The role is theirs on every device of theirs. Their row is never removed or demoted, and only they sign out a device of theirs.
- A token carries its person's role (`superadmin` off that table, else `members.role`), read on every request, so a role change bites on the next call. There can be several owners.
- Who may do what is one table: `can(role, action)` in `roles.ts`. Every gated route asks it; `/api/meta` answers the caller's row as `you.can` and the app hides by it (`may` / `mayOn`, `src/cloud.ts`). An empty `can` on a status means not yet known: `mayOn` hides the control until the domain answers, and the iPhone's `CloudStatus.may` shows it, since what it gates includes editing, which works offline. Change a permission there, nowhere else.
- A member is not a limited account: `PUT /api/manifest` isn't role-gated. Owners = invite, remove, sign out, roles, other people's agents, every public address. The superadmin alone = bind, wipe, the update card (`workspace.manage`), and a new code for somebody who has signed in before (`people.recover`).
- Nobody removes or demotes their own row, or signs out the device the call is made with.
- Codes are 100 bits (Crockford base32): no throttle, lockout or counter. Revoking a row bites on the next request.
- `last_seen_at` (beat) and `last_used_at` (agent request) move at most hourly.

## People

- A member is keyed by normalized email (`m-` + 8 hex): re-inviting an address reaches the same person. Member ids enter the manifest, emails never.
- **A removed person's row is never deleted** (`members.disabled`): out of the People list and unable to sign in, still in the directory so what they wrote keeps their name. Inviting the address again is the same person, a member, with their Private; restricted folders they were in are not given back.
- The address is never verified, so a code is whoever redeems it. Once somebody has signed in as a person (`lastSeenAt` set — a redeem or a trade sets it — or the superadmin's row), a new code for them is only theirs or the superadmin's to make: the superadmin controls the account the workspace is stored on and can read all of it anyway. Before that the row is an invitation any owner may re-issue (`mayInviteAgain`, `src/cloud.ts` ⇄ `mayInviteAgain`, the iPhone's `People.swift` ⇄ `createInvite`, `api.ts`: **change all three**).
- Invite: the owner's device mints `dkln-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX` and sends only its sha256; 7 days default; cap `MAX_DAYS` ⇄ `MAX_INVITE_TTL_MS`, **change both**. A Mac and an iPhone mint in the engine (`invite.rs`), a browser on the page (`src/web/codes.ts`): the alphabet, the prefixes (`dkln`, `dkla`), the two lines and the lifetimes are written in both, `invite.rs`'s tests read `codes.ts`, and `verify-harness/webcodes.test.mjs` holds what a browser hashes to what the worker hashes back. **Change both.** Line: `doklin-invite <endpoint> <code>`. `normalize_code` ⇄ `normalizeCode` (`layout.ts`) must match exactly: **change both**. The browser's sign-in reads a paste with `parsePasted` (`src/web/SignIn.tsx`) ⇄ `parse_pasted`: **change both**.
- Redeem runs first (`/api/meta` is behind the gate): `cloud_redeem`, then probe, `cloud_join`. The code is spent at redeem; later failures retry the probe only.
- `CloudPeople.tsx` asks (`cloud_people`), never stores: no role is on disk. Everyone reads the list; lists a role may not act on come back empty.
- `cloud_join(endpoint, token)` asks for no folder: the workspace lands in `~/Doklin/<name>` (`Name 2` when taken).
- An owner's unused codes go with them: removing or demoting an owner withdraws **every** pending invite, device codes among them. Otherwise a removed owner returns on a code they kept.

## Devices

- A device (a Mac, an iPhone, a browser) is a row in `tokens`: one sign-in, one device. It gets there by `POST /api/auth/join` — a browser by `POST /api/web/session` — and no other way: with an invite somebody made for its person, or a **device code** that person made for themselves (`cloud_device_code` → `POST /api/auth/devices/code`).
- To the domain a device code is that person's pending invite: same table, same grammar, same line (`doklin-invite <endpoint> <code>`), same redeem, one pending code a person. Never give it a prefix, a table or a route of its own to redeem.
- **No token or key is ever shown or carried between two devices.** Lost every device: a member asks the superadmin for a new code; the superadmin recovers with the Cloudflare account ([cloud.md](cloud.md) *Flows*).
- Everyone lists and signs out their own (`devices.own`); an owner anybody's but the superadmin's (`people.remove`; `409` for one of theirs). Somebody else's is a `404` to a member.
- Signing a device out is a removal (`removed_tokens`: it deletes its copy). A device leaving is not: `DELETE /api/auth/session` forgets the row and says nothing. Disconnect calls it, bounded by `END_SESSION_WAIT` and never waited on to succeed; a wipe does not (it empties the table).
- `DEVICE_CODE_MINUTES` (`invite.rs`) is the lifetime; the worker's `MAX_DEVICE_CODE_TTL_MS` is looser only to absorb clock skew.
- A join whose open folder carries that workspace's marker offers *Resume* (`CloudSetup.tsx`): the only way a disconnected Mac's folder syncs again, since nobody can type a token for it.

## A browser (`web.ts`, `auth.ts`)

- A browser holds no copy of the workspace and builds no manifest. It reads what a Mac reads (poll, manifests, stored copies) and the worker writes what it changes, through `commit`, signed with the person's member id.
- Its token starts `dkw_` (`BROWSER_TOKEN_PREFIX`) and exists only in the cookie `__Host-doklin` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict): no body ever carries it, so no script ever holds it. `dkw_…` as a bearer is nobody, and so is any other token as the cookie — **what a browser may reach is a property of the token, not of how it arrived.**
- The cookie counts only on a request the app's own script made (`ownScript`: the browser's own `Sec-Fetch-*` headers). A cookie goes with every request to its site — a public page's `<img>`, a page on another host of the zone — so this check, and the page policy ([cloud-publishing.md](cloud-publishing.md) *What a page may do*), are what the sign-in rests on. The route that signs in asks the same of its caller.
- A browser's sign-in is answered on `/api/web/*` and the routes `ALSO` lists, and nothing else: no manifest or blob `PUT`, no version store, no bind, wipe or plan. **Adding a route to `ALSO`: it must change nothing on `GET`.**
- People, devices and agents are in `ALSO`: a browser is the only device some people have, so it invites, makes device codes and agents' login codes, signs devices out and removes people, each by its person's role as on a Mac (`can`, on the same routes). **So signing a browser out ends that browser and nothing it started.** A device code it made is a Mac's sign-in to whoever redeems it, and outlasts it; what a browser let in is a row under Devices or Agents, signed out there. A browser's narrow token still keeps script from holding a credential and keeps a browser from writing a manifest; it does not keep whoever has the use of a signed-in browser from staying.
- Restricted folders are in `ALSO` too: making one, its people, deleting it, each the route a Mac calls and answered to the people in the folder alone. A Mac makes a folder into a restricted one on its own disk and syncs the result; a browser makes the folder, then moves the old one's files to its top (`POST /api/web/move`, `to.path` `""`). **That is two steps and, past 16 files, several requests: until the last answers, part of the folder is still where everyone who could open it before can.** The browser says how many files went when it stops early, and takes the new folder back when none did (`Saving.restrict`).
- `/api/web/*` is a person's, with handlers of its own, never routed to the document API: no scopes, no `dry`, no `force`, no marker or anchor check (a thread is a person's to move or remove), the sidecar written whole, the home page theirs to choose. What both do to the manifest is shared and never copied: `commit.ts`, `changes.ts`, `publishing.ts`, `searchIn`.
- A section is named by its id and a path is as that section stores it; no folder name stands for a section.
- **A note and its sidecar are one save** (`PUT /api/web/doc`): one manifest write, each file over the `rev` that was read (`null`: must not exist), and when either moved on neither is written (`409 {part, rev}`). The worker merges nothing: the browser reads the other version and merges ([architecture.md](architecture.md) *The browser version*).
- A change to documents answers the manifest it was made to (`over`) beside the one it left (`etag`), so the browser that made it does not read the manifest again.
- A browser beats like any device (`POST /api/poll`): it is in presence under a name of its own ("Chrome on Windows", the name it signed in with), and the document it is editing is its lease. All of one browser's tabs are one device.
- The page is `/app`, and everything of it is under `/app`, where no public page can be (`RESERVED_SLUGS`). Its policy is the reverse of a public page's: script runs, from the domain's own files only, and the page talks to the domain alone and may be framed by nobody (a public page may frame anything). An html document's own script runs in `/app/frame`, sandboxed: an origin that is nobody's, sent no sign-in. The client is [architecture.md](architecture.md) *The browser version*.
- A sign-in that ends — signed out there, signed out by an owner, the person removed — is answered with the cookie's removal and `Clear-Site-Data: "cache", "storage"`: the browser's version of a Mac deleting its copy. Stored copies are served `private, immutable` and `Vary: Cookie`, so one read under a sign-in is not answered from the cache under another or none.

## Losing access (`engine.rs`, `mod.rs`)

- **Only `401 {removed: true}` deletes** (`RemoteError::Removed`). Never infer it from another refusal: a D1 outage answers a bare `401` to every member at once.
- The engine exits `Exit::Removed`; `access_removed` then deletes the folder outright (not the Trash), the local version stores (the workspace's and each section's) and the sync state, drops the `cloud.json` entry and emits `cloud-removed`. Nothing in the cloud is deleted.
- `remove_copy` deletes only a folder whose marker names that domain and `wsId`, resolved through symlinks, and never a path that holds the home directory.
- Every device obeys it, the superadmin's included.
- A removal never lapses: an offline Mac deletes when it next reaches the domain. A wipe empties `removed_tokens`, so it deletes nobody's copy.
- `cloud_removed` returns the removals since launch, for a window that was not listening; a window dedupes by `root` + `at`.

## Spaces (`spaces.ts`, `addresses.ts`)

What a Mac does with them: [cloud.md](cloud.md) *Sections*.

- A space (restricted folder, or a person's Private) is a workspace of its own under `spaces/<id>/`: the sync handlers run unchanged on an `Env` whose bucket is that prefix (`spaceEnv`). **One gate** (`openSpace`) sits before everything under `/api/spaces/<id>`; a new route goes behind it, never beside it.
- **Outside a space it does not exist**, for owners too: same `404` as an unknown id, no name, no count. Presence shows a person is here without the path; leases in it are not listed.
- Nothing about a space is in the shared manifest, the shared blobs or the shared version store.
- Everyone in a restricted folder is equal. The last person cannot leave (`409`); a folder whose last person was removed from the workspace stays stored with nobody in it.
- A Private's id is its person's (`p-<member hex>`), so it cannot be made twice; it never has a second person.
- `spaces.etag` mirrors the space's manifest etag so one poll covers every space. It is written after each manifest write through the worker (`noteEtag`; `commit.ts` too) and repaired by any manifest read that finds it stale. A writer that bypasses both leaves other Macs blind.
- An owner (`pages.manage`) is told of every public address and can take any page down, including one in a space they are not in — and of such a page only what its address tells anyone (`everyPage.ts`). Nothing else about a space reaches an owner.
- A public address is one space's for the whole domain: `addresses.slug` is the primary key, claimed before the manifest is stored and released after (`claimAddresses`, called by `putManifest` and `commit`). The registry may hold a claim its space failed to store, never lack one it stored. The shared workspace's claims are best effort (its sync never waits on D1), so a space's claim is also checked against the shared map.

## Presence and leases (`presence.ts`, `engine.rs`)

- The beat rides `POST /api/poll`; `GET` writes nothing. Owed every 45 s (`PRESENCE_BEAT`) at the most — every poll, once polls are a minute or more apart (*Engine behaviour*, docs/cloud.md) — and on a new edited path; a failed poll keeps it owed, no early retry. Only a new path, a cycle, the edited document's lease freeing, a focus after a quiet spell, or a take-over bring a poll forward.
- A beat says how long it holds: `ttl` = 2.5 × the gap to the next beat, which the worker clamps to 90 s–15 min (`PRESENCE_TTL_MS` is the floor and the default). A presence row and the lease it claims both live until `until` = now + ttl, so a device polling every five minutes stays listed as here, not editing; an idle beat names no path and lets its leases go.
- "Editing" = opened or typed in within 5 min (`ACTIVITY_FRESH`); `cloud_set_activity` fires per tab switch, never per key.
- A beat naming a path claims its lease in one upsert (free, lapsed or already this Mac's; `take: true` regardless) and releases this Mac's others. Key = space + path lowercased (`lease_key` ⇄ `leaseKey`, **change both**). No reaper: reads filter, beats sweep.
- **Advisory, always**: nothing that writes the manifest reads a lease, agents included. A leased document opens view-only elsewhere (`LeaseBanner.tsx`); merges cover the gaps.
- Any failure (no DB, D1 error, failed poll, paused engine) shows no lock; never on the sync path.

## Agents

- An agent (`a-` + 8 hex) is a name, scopes and the person it belongs to (`agents.member_id`); not a member. The sync cycle has no agent code; the app's agent calls are `HttpRemote` methods, off the `Remote` trait.
- **An agent sees exactly what its person sees**: Library, their Private, the restricted folders they are in. Its token's `Auth.memberId` is its person, so every "is this person in this space" is the question a Mac's token is asked, on every request: added to a folder, taken out, the agent follows on its next call. Scopes limit what it does there, never where. Nothing else widens or narrows it.
- Anyone has agents of their own (`agents.own`). Only its person changes one or mints it a login code: a code is a way in as that person, the reason an invite code for somebody who has signed in is theirs to make. An owner (`agents.manage`) is listed everybody's and may remove one or log a machine out, nothing more; to anyone else another person's agent is a `404` on the agents routes.
- **An agent's name, and whose it is, are everybody's**: the directory on `GET /api/meta` lists each agent's id, name and `person` to everyone in the workspace — nothing more (no scopes, machines or codes) — so an app says what an agent changed as "Maya's Research agent". Changing or removing one stays its person's and owners'.
- Removing a person deletes their agents. Inviting them again brings none back.
- `publish` is off unless ticked (a remote agent can't be asked to confirm). Scopes are per route (`ROUTES`, `agentApi.ts`). The home page is set only in the app.
- **The terminal** (`terminal.rs`): on a Mac the app logs its own `doklin` in to each connected workspace as an agent of the person's, `<name>'s terminal (<Mac>)`, with all four scopes (the person is at the keyboard). It is found by that name among their own agents (`mine`), never remembered — Disconnect forgets the `cloud.json` entry, and a remembered id would make a second agent on reconnect — else made. The code is minted like any other and piped to `doklin login <endpoint> -`, never put in argv; the CLI's `login` stays the one writer of `credentials.json`. A workspace `doklin whoami -w` already answers for is left alone. It runs on *Install Command Line Tool* (every connected workspace; the dialog says how each went) and after connect, join and resume when the terminal runs this app's `doklin`. Disconnect leaves the login.
- The only way in: a `dkla-…` code (`AGENT_PREFIX` ⇄ `normalizeAgentCode`, **change both**), valid 1 h (`AGENT_CODE_MINUTES`; the worker's looser cap absorbs clock skew), shown once in `buildAgentLoginPrompt` (install line + `doklin login <endpoint> <code>`; the iPhone's `AgentPrompt` writes the same words, **change both**). Login deletes the code and mints the machine's `dka_` token in one statement.
- New work goes under `Private/` unless the person names a place — by instruction only: the login prompt, `doklin help agent|sections` and the CLI's login message say so, and say that every path starts with its section. Never a setting, a flag or a default folder: a path says where a thing is, and has to.
- *Copy instructions for agents* (Settings, `AGENT_INSTRUCTIONS`) is pasted once into any agent's chat; the agent saves its note in the instructions file it loads into every conversation, after asking its person whether that is the global file or this project's own. The files it names (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md` and their global paths) are examples: it works for an agent it does not name, which uses its own file, or says where to paste the note when it cannot write one. That note rides in every conversation, so it stays a few lines and points at `doklin help agent` for the rest. It is where "dok" is taught: one of Doklin's documents.

## Document API

- **Paths are literal** (`agentPlace.ts`), the ones on the person's disk, and **every path starts with its section**: `Library/…` the shared section, `Private/…` their Private, `Restricted/<folder>/…` a folder they are in, the names matched in any case. **A path with no section is refused** (`400`) with the sentence that says which to use — "Projects/plan is in no section. Say which: Library/Projects/plan (everyone), Private/Projects/plan (only your person) or Restricted/<folder>/Projects/plan." — on every route: nothing reaches everyone by being left unsaid. `Library/Library/…`, `Library/Private/…` and `Library/Restricted/…` are in no section either; the shared manifest refuses them (`RESERVED_FOLDERS`).
- A call runs in the section its path names, on that section's manifest mounted at its folder (`mount.ts`): every lookup, refusal and answer carries the path as typed, and `commit` takes the change back to the stored form — relative to the section, the shared one still the bucket root (space `""`) — before it is checked. A folder the person is not in answers what a folder nobody made answers.
- A restricted folder is called what a Mac calls it: `folderNames` ⇄ `candidates` (`space.rs`), `folderName` ⇄ `sanitize_folder_name` (`config.rs`). **Change both.** The worker hands the names out in the order the folders were made, a Mac in the order they reached it, so of two folders with one name a Mac may have the other first; `doklin ls Restricted` is what the agent calls each.
- An agent makes, renames and deletes no section and changes nobody's membership; `Library`, `Private` and `Restricted/<folder>` themselves do not move. A section's own folder as a move's destination means into it, and published it is the whole section (`Library/` is everything everyone sees).
- A move between sections (`moveAcross`, `changes.ts`) is the one change that is two manifest writes: ≤ 16 files, written where they go first under the ids they had (a section that already holds one gives it a new one), then removed with a tombstone `to` — what a Mac's own cross-section move leaves (`WorkerTx::moved_to`, `tests.rs`). So links to them keep opening them. A file that changed in between stays where it is. A page is given up by the write that removes its file and claimed after by the section it went to; one that could not be is answered as `unpublished`.
- The workspace itself (`""`) is a place to list and search, and holds no document: its listing is the sections the person has (`Library`, `Private`, `Restricted` when they are in a folder), never a file. A deep listing, a search and the page list cover every section, Library among them, opening ≤ 8 spaces a request beside it (`MAX_SECTIONS_READ`) and naming the ones they left out.
- Stopping a page takes a path or an address (`deletePage`): a word that is in no section is tried as an address, in whichever section holds it, and is told it needs a section when none does.
- `find` is where a link to a page leads (`getFind`; the link's format: [cloud.md](cloud.md) *Links*): workspace, space and file id in, the path as this person names it out. Another workspace, a space they are not in, a file gone and one never made are one `404`.
- A document's addresses are in `info` alone — its public ones, and `linkTo` (workspace, space, the file people open: md, else html), never a listing: an agent lists far more than it hands on. The worker answers the ids, never the link; the CLI writes it (`cli/src/link.rs`).
- **The worker is a third writer** (`commit.ts`): read manifest + etag → apply (checks `if-rev` against that manifest; a refusal writes nothing) → `validateManifest` → blobs create-only → manifest `onlyIf etagMatches`; a lost race redoes it. The precondition is the file's `rev`, never the etag. An unchanged manifest isn't written.
- `apply_remote` relies on: new file `f-` + 12 hex, `rev: 1`; edit `rev + 1`, new hash/size/mtime; move new path, `rev + 1`, same hash and mtime; removal a tombstone `{path, rev + 1, ts, by}`; a write over its own tombstone takes the next rev; `seq + 1`; `by` = agent id, or the member id of a person in a browser; a document's files in one write. `WorkerTx` (`tests.rs`) mirrors `Tx`: **change both**. Macs apply it next poll, writing nothing back.
- A **document** = a stem with up to two versions, markdown (`.md .markdown .mdown .mkd`) and html; its sidecar `<stem>.meta.jsonl` never crosses the API. Reads default to md, else html; writes default to md and never guess on html-only. Nothing else crosses the API: a path outside `syncs` (`documents.ts`) is refused, so `tree` answers `{dir, folders, documents}`.
- Refused (would sync to nobody): hidden segments, `IGNORED_NAMES` (⇄ `is_hidden_or_ignored` in engine/src/lib.rs ⇄ `isHiddenOrIgnored` in the iPhone's `Files.swift`, **change all three**), the `.doklin-sync-tmp` suffix, sidecars. A name under an existing folder in another case takes its spelling.
- No silent overwrite: `if-rev: <n>`, `if-rev: *` or `force=1`. Same bytes: `unchanged`. `htmlBehind`: html mtime > 2 s older than md.
- A public entry follows its document: its md file, else its html.
- Comments: reads keep markers (`clean=1` strips) so read-then-write keeps them. **A content write never moves a thread** (`markersKept`, `anchorsKept`): md must carry exactly the old markers, compared as written (moving one with its words is fine); html must leave no thread with less of its place than the file had (`placeOf`, `htmlText.ts`: 2 its words — as written, folded, or reworded a little — 1 its part by tag and opening words, or by its own id when it has no words, 0 neither). Else `400 comments`, `--force` included — threads change only through the comments routes. New md threads need words occurring once in plain text. An agent's html thread is on one element, by its opening words or `#id`: `{path: "", tag, text, ids}`; the app's are also on words (`quote`, `prefix`, `suffix`; `HtmlAnchor`, `src/htmlComments.ts`). **The content decides where a thread is**: ids and `path` only say where to look first, a find that is not exact is said so, and finding a thread never rewrites its anchor. The app finds a place in the page as drawn (`htmlBridge.ts`); the worker and the iPhone read the file as written, so a level 0 on a page that runs script, or for an anchor with nothing to check by, is "can't tell", never "gone" (`known`). Stamps are to the second; finer would make every Mac rewrite the sidecar.
- **Links between documents** (`agentLinks.ts`; the rules are `src/docLinks.ts`'s). A read shows each link that has a record (`link`, the sidecar) where its document is now, a named one by its name now, as the agent's person can open them; anything else as stored. A write keeps each link's record under the destination it now has, whether as stored or as read, and taken back to the form the note has (words the agent changed stay its own). A link the stored note didn't have gets a record by its path in the person's view, in any section; then what a save writes decides (`linksToSave`), so a link into a section other than the note's own and Library is stored as it was, unless new. Text and sidecar are one `commit`. The marker check compares with the text as read, the text as stored passing too. Where a link sits in the text is `noteLinks.ts`'s (marked's lexer, inline links only; a note with `\r\n` is left as written).
- `links` (`getLinks`): `from` is each link of the markdown to another document, its words as a read shows them, and the document as `doklin` names it or `closed` (a deleted file, another section and one never made alike); `to` is the documents linking to it among those the person can open, from D1 `links` (*Search*), caught up first. A document they can't open is one that doesn't exist. An html version's links are not listed.
- **Tags** (`tagList.ts`, `agentTags.ts`, `webTags.ts`; the rules are `src/tags.ts`'s, a note's lines [architecture.md](architecture.md) *Tags*). The list is the shared manifest's `tags`, whichever section a call is in, and a change of it is a `commit` of that manifest over the list as it is then: the later change of one tag wins, as a device's queued one does. A markdown write (`PUT doc`, so `edit` too) is held to it where it adds or changes a listed tag's line, compared with the text as stored: one value, one of the tag's (`tag_value`); a line left as it was, or removed, is never checked. A person's writes in a browser are not checked. `POST tag` is `writeTag` over the stored text in one `commit`, each tag and value as the list spells them, new lines in the list's order; never on html-only documents. `GET tags` is `{tags}`, each with its `desc`. An agent only adds (`POST tags`: a new tag at the end, or values at the end of a tag's); renaming and removing are a person's, in the app or a browser (`PUT`/`DELETE /api/web/tags`), and nothing the worker does rewrites notes: the app renames them.
- `tree` and `search` take `where=name:value`, or `where=name` for any value (≤ 8, ANDed, names refused unless listed), and answer `where` back as the list spells it — the CLI's sign that the worker knows tags. A listing's notes carry `tags` (`{name, value, known}`, the list's order) from D1 `tags` as it is, with `tagsBehind` when a section's index isn't whole at its manifest; with `where` it catches up first (`202`, as a search) and has no folders. `info` reads `tags` from the note's text.
- No history, presence, lease or sync in the CLI: undo is the Macs' version store.
- The iPhone writes comments into its own copy by the same rules (`placeForMarker`, `anchorProblem`, `threadsOf` here; `src/criticMarkup.ts`, `src/metaFile.ts` on the Mac): its port (`ios/DoklinKit/Sources/DoklinKit/Comments/`) and the TS are held to one set of cases, `verify-harness/comments-fixture.json` (`comments.test.mjs` for the TS, `CommentsTests` for Swift, the engine's sidecar tests for an anchor's bytes, `drive.mjs` for the app's script on `places`). **Change all of them together.**

## Search (`search.ts`)

- `search_text`: per document, lower-cased text (markers or tags stripped) + comment bodies; too big → `notSearched`. A search catches up first (≤ 8 inline, else `202` after ≤ 36). API writes catch up via `ctx.waitUntil`; a Mac's PUT never does (no D1 write per save). Query `instr(folded, ?) > 0`.
- A document's name (file name, extension and folders not included) matches from the manifest, never the mirror: a rename needs no catch-up, a name-only hit (`inName`, no lines) costs no blob read, and a too-big document found by name is a result, not `notSearched`. Named documents sort first. The Mac (`search_workspace`) and the iPhone (`WorkspaceFiles.search`) match the same name, html-only pages included.
- One mirror per space (`space_id`, `''` shared), keyed `(space_id, fid)`: a file that changes section is in both manifests for a moment, and one section's catch-up must not drop the other's row. A search scans `space_id IN (…)` — the sections its person is in — and never another space's rows.
- The same catch-up fills `links` (`targetsOf`): for each markdown note, the files it links to, by its records and by *Copy links* of this workspace, else by path but only into its own section or Library (a restricted folder's name is each person's, so a path naming one is not followed). `linkersOf` answers `links --to`; `search_state.held` is current for every table.
- And `tags` (`pairsOf`): every pair of each markdown note's block, not only listed tags', so a tag added to the list later needs no re-read — for each name, lower-cased, the first line keyed by it when that line is a pair, the value as spelled and `folded`. A note too big to mirror has none. Tags narrow a search (`taggedIn`) beside the text or alone; alone, every such note is a result with no lines, no limit and no blob read, and a too-big note under the folder is `notSearched`. The browser's search sends `tag=` (same forms, names already the list's) with `q` empty.

## CLI (`cli/`)

- Thin: never names a sidecar, pairs md/html or builds a manifest.
- Token only in `~/.config/doklin/credentials.json` (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME`), 0600 in 0700, one entry per domain; `-w` picks. Beside it, kept the same way, `usage.json`: `{"<domain>": {day, requests, reportedAt, limits}}` — this machine's count today and the domain's last `limits` (*Limits*). Trap: the app runs the bundled `doklin` with the app's environment, so an `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` set only in a shell's startup files is not the one its login writes under.
- `login <domain> -` takes the code from the first line piped in — how the app hands one over.
- Certificates are the machine's to trust, as for every program on it (`http.rs` `tls`): the OS's own check on a Mac, as in the apps ([cloud.md](cloud.md) rule 12); elsewhere the system's CA store, or `SSL_CERT_FILE` / `SSL_CERT_DIR` in its place, as curl and git read them. No roots are bundled. A proxy in `HTTPS_PROXY` / `NO_PROXY` is followed, as curl does. An unknown issuer's refusal says how to trust one there (Keychain Access, or `SSL_CERT_FILE`), so an agent behind a proxy that re-signs HTTPS can act on it.
- Exit codes are a contract: `0` ok · `1` other · `2` usage · `3` not found · `4` not permitted · `5` conflict · `6` not logged in/revoked · `7` worker too old (an unknown route's 404/405, or a `--where` answered without `where`) · `8` the domain asked to slow down, or today's Cloudflare requests are used up (a second `429`, this machine's allowance, Cloudflare's 1027 page) · `9` this doklin is older than the domain answers (`410`, *Mandatory releases* in [release.md](release.md)): the worker's sentence carries the install line, so an agent updates and runs the command again.
- JSON when stdout isn't a TTY or with `--json`; `read` puts content on stdout, metadata on stderr. `--dry-run` on every change; re-asks on `202`.
- Every word after `doklin` is a command on every system; an unknown one is exit `2`, `doklin` alone is the overview. `open` is the one command about this Mac: it hands paths on this disk, and links, to the app (refused off a Mac, needs no login) and never knows workspaces — the app places them.
- A link to a page is taken wherever a document is named (`TAKE_LINKS`, `main.rs`): one `GET find` turns it into the path before the command runs, in the link's workspace; `-w` naming another is exit `2`.
- One `doklin` per Mac. The app carries it at `Doklin.app/Contents/Helpers/doklin` (`bundle.macOS.files`); *Doklin → Install Command Line Tool* (`cli_install.rs`) links `/usr/local/bin/doklin` to it, replacing what is there and naming a `doklin` the terminal finds first. `CLI_INSTALL_LINE` (`scripts/install-doklin.sh`) is for everything else; on a Mac with the app it links to that copy instead of downloading.
- Concepts: `doklin help agent|sections|documents|comments|tags|publishing`. A topic wins over a command of its name (`help comments`, `help tags`); `<command> --help` is the command's.
- Paths go to the worker as typed, and the sections are the worker's to read: the CLI has no check of the worker's version for them. `publish` and `published` call the whole shared section "the folder Library".

## Limits

- **Only a client that doesn't send protects the budget.** Workers Free is 100,000 requests a day for the whole account (reset 00:00 UTC, then Cloudflare's 1027 page for everything), and a request the worker refuses has already been counted. So each client counts its own requests before sending, per domain per UTC day (`floor(ms / 86_400_000)`, the worker's day): the engine at `HttpRemote::auth`, kept in the workspace's state dir; the CLI in `usage.json` (*CLI*). The worker keeps no daily count; it sums what clients report.
- **The numbers have one source**, `cloud-worker/src/limits.ts`, keyed by the plan: D1 `settings.plan`, `free` (default) or `paid`, changed by `PUT /api/settings/plan` (`workspace.manage`: the superadmin). They reach clients as `limits` — `/api/meta` signed in, every poll, `GET /api/agent/me`, `login`'s `201` — and each keeps the last answer: no client has numbers of its own. `null` = no such limit (Workers Paid). D1 down = the Free numbers, `fleetToday: null`.
- **A device** counts all its requests today, not only bulk ones. Past `deviceBulk` its bulk lane waits until 00:00 UTC — its person may lift that, for that device, for the day; past `deviceCeiling` it goes `slow`, which can't be lifted. Edits always go out. How: [cloud.md](cloud.md) *Engine behaviour*.
- **The fleet**: a client sends `x-doklin-used: <requests today>` at most every 15 min; the worker upserts D1 `usage` by token id in `ctx.waitUntil` and answers `limits.fleetToday`, today's sum. Past `fleetBulkWaits` every device's bulk waits, past `fleetSlow` every device is `slow`; neither can be lifted. Self-reported, so a wrong number can only slow devices down. Public page views and other Workers on the account are not in it: the thresholds leave that room.
- **The worker's loop catchers**: two `[[ratelimits]]` bindings checked right after `authenticate`, `DEVICE_LIMIT` (600 / 60 s per device token) and `AGENT_LIMIT` (120 / 60 s per agent token), in the `wrangler.toml` the update script and the setup prompt write. Over → `429` + `Retry-After: 60`. Approximate (a burst gets a few more through) and a signal, never the budget's guard. A binding the env lacks is not checked.
- **Cloudflare's daily limit** is Cloudflare's page, not the worker's: its status isn't documented, so it is known by its body — not JSON, and containing `1027`. A device goes `limited` until 00:00 UTC; the CLI exits `8` at once.
- **The CLI's allowance**: `limits.cliDaily` requests a machine, per domain per UTC day. Every request is counted before it goes, a retry, a login and a logout included; past the allowance nothing is sent (exit `8`, saying when it comes back in local time). No `limits` known yet — before a login has answered — means no allowance. A `429` waits `Retry-After` (≤ 60 s, `MAX_WAIT`) and is asked once more, then exit `8`. `x-doklin-used` rides a signed-in request at most every 15 min (`reportedAt`), never a `login` route's: it holds no token yet, or the one going away, whose row would go on counting today beside the new one's.
- Trap: a spent allowance refuses `logout` and a new `login` too, until 00:00 UTC. The app's Agents list can still log that machine out.
- Other Free-plan caps: 50 subrequests and 10 ms CPU a request, D1 5M rows read / 100k written a day. Hence batches and `202`s.
- An agent write costs ~`1 + 2 × Macs online` requests. Watch: `scripts/cloud-usage.sh <domain> [days]`. Unmeasured: write CPU at 5,000 files. Its person, or an owner, removes a runaway agent.
